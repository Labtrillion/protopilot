Tennessee Eastman Challenge Process

A plant that shuts itself down in hours if nobody holds it

Freeze every valve of the Tennessee Eastman plant at its published design point and nothing seems to happen for two hours. At 3.59 h the reactor pressure reaches its 3000 kPa g interlock and the plant stops. This app runs the benchmark Downs and Vogel published in 1993, ported line by line to JavaScript from its public Fortran code, and lets you close its loops a layer at a time: the inventories, then the compositions the analyzers report late, then production and the product mix, then an override that gives up production before the interlock gives up everything.

1. Overview

This guide covers the Tennessee Eastman Challenge Process: five units (reactor, condenser, vapor–liquid separator, recycle compressor, product stripper), eight components A to H, four reactions, 50 states, 12 manipulated variables (XMV), 41 measurements (XMEAS) and 20 disturbances (IDV). The plant is the public model. The control structures are N. L. Ricker's published decentralized structure, read from his archived Simulink model, an inventory-only subset of it, this app's pressure override, and an MPC built for this app. The shutdown limits and prices are the benchmark's own teaching values, not data from any plant.

For faculty

Section 2 is lecture background, section 3 is what to demonstrate live, and the scenario table in section 3 is assignable as-is. Section 5 says what not to claim.

For students

Start at section 3, run the app while you read, then come back to section 4 to connect what you saw to the equations. Nothing here is graded. The app is.

For practitioners

Section 2 places Tennessee Eastman Challenge Process against the real unit operation, section 4 gives the model you would have to replace, and section 5 is the honest gap list.

The one-paragraph version

The base case is a steady state that is not stable: with every valve fixed the reactor pressure trips the interlock at 3.59 h (case 1). The inventory loops hold it until a feed composition changes. After the A/C step the pressure trips at 5.84 h because nothing moves the feeds (case 2). Ricker's seventeen loops ride the same step on analyzers that report every 6 minutes, 6 minutes late (cases 2 and 4). The purge is the largest part of the operating cost, so raising the pressure setpoint from 2705 to 2800 kPa g cuts the cost under a random feed from 176.1 to 146.3 $/h (case 5) and moves the plant toward its interlock. When the A feed is lost, a rate cut with the pressure override keeps the plant running where the same cut without it trips (case 8).

2. Background

The engineering problem

Gaseous reactants A, C, D and E react in a stirred, cooled reactor to the liquid products G and H, with a by-product F, B is an inert that enters with the A and C feed. The reactor effluent is cooled and separated: the vapor goes back to the reactor through the recycle compressor, a small purge removes the inert, and the liquid goes to a stripper where the A and C feed strips the light components back into the recycle. The product leaves the bottom of the stripper.

The plant has twelve valves, and almost every one affects every unit through the recycle. Two products must be made in a specified ratio at a specified rate, the compositions are measured by three analyzers that sample every 0.1 or 0.25 h and report one period late, and an interlock stops the plant when the reactor pressure, the reactor temperature or one of three liquid levels leaves its band.

Where it sits in control theory

Inventories first. A gas with no outlet but the purge accumulates, and liquid levels on pumped outlets do not regulate themselves. Until every inventory has a loop, the plant drifts. Once each has one, it stays up.

Compositions next. An inventory loop moves the outlet of its own vessel. When a feed changes composition, someone must move the other feeds, and the only measurements of composition are sampled analyzers.

One production handle. Ricker's structure scales every flow setpoint by a production index, so one loop on the product flow moves the whole plant together, and the mix loop shifts D against E through a feedforward from the mix setpoint.

Economics and protection. The cost function charges for the purge, the product losses, compressor work and steam. Pushing the pressure toward its limit lowers the purge losses. An override decides in advance what gives way when the limit is approached.

What this app teaches

  • Tell a steady state from a stable one, and find the inventories a plant does not regulate itself
  • Build a plantwide structure in layers: inventories, compositions, production and quality
  • Read and use sampled, delayed analyzer measurements
  • Move a whole plant with one production handle
  • Relate a setpoint to an operating cost through the published cost function
  • Recognize a stiction limit cycle and what tuning can and cannot do about it
  • Explain what an override gives up, and recognize an upset no structure rides through

The application in practice

Downs and Vogel posed the problem in 1990 and published it in 1993 as a test bed for plantwide control. It has since been used for decentralized structures, self-optimizing control, MPC and fault detection. The fault-detection data sets distributed by Braatz and coworkers were generated with it. The structure here follows Ricker's 1996 decentralized design.

Automation and its broader implications

Chemical plants are run by structures like this one: hundreds of PI loops arranged so that inventories are held, a few handles set production and quality, and overrides protect the limits. Getting the structure right matters more than tuning any single loop.

3. Operating the app

The model's own explicit Euler step of 1 s, the controllers every step, a log row every minute. The clock is in hours. The speed ladder runs from 0.05 to 4 hours of plant per second, and Chrome sustains the top speed: the 48 h challenge runs in 12.0 s. Changing the speed never changes the trajectory. The header's Manual is the all-Manual structure: every valve on its station, every loop open. Auto runs the structure chosen in Structure (in Auto), the first control of the first card. A valve the chosen structure does not drive stays on its station, which stays live in Auto.

Interface anatomy

The Plant view draws the flowsheet in the topology of Downs and Vogel's Fig. 1, numbered as the paper numbers it, so that each stream and instrument can be found with the paper in hand. A circle is a measurement, with its XMEAS number and its reading beside it, a square on a valve's stem is its XMV number, and the bowtie fills with the valve's opening (XMV 12, the agitator, is the square beside the motor), and a diamond on a line is the stream number (1–3 the A, D and E feeds, 4 the A and C feed to the stripper, 5 the stripper overhead, 6 the reactor feed, 7 the reactor product through the condenser, 8 the compressor discharge, 9 the purge, 10 the separator liquid, 11 the product). The violet boxes are the three analyzers with their XMEAS ranges: the reactor feed (23–28), the purge (29–36) and the product (37–41). The recycle valve, XMV 5, is the spillback from the compressor's discharge to its suction. The purge leaves the suction side. Flow dashes move with the flows and the three liquid levels are drawn on their transmitters' spans. Where a reading has no room beside its circle (a phone, a 1024 px laptop) the circle stays and the readings the interlock watches, with the production, are listed under the drawing. The Limits view shows the reactor pressure, level and temperature as dials with their interlock zones (red) and the override threshold (amber), with the limits written under them, and the separator and stripper levels as bars on their whole interlock span, the trip levels beside the red lines and the transmitter's 0–100 % span dashed inside. The Cost and mix view splits the present operating cost into its terms, marks the mean since t = 0 on the same bar, and draws the product G share by mass against its target band. The operating cost trends draw the mean since t = 0 dashed beside the present cost: under a random feed the present cost swings by more than the differences the cases compare.

Each loop is a card, on the views it belongs to: production (with the structure list and the operating point) and the product mix first, then pressure and the override, the reactor feed analyzer trims, the temperatures and levels, and the seven flow loops. The disturbance switches are at the foot of the control panel: IDV(1) and IDV(8) in Beginner, all twenty in Advanced.

First run, step by step

  1. Watch it trip. Case 1, Manual: 3.59 h to the pressure interlock.

  2. Close the inventories. Case 2: stable until the A/C step, then a trip at 5.84 h.

  3. Move the plant. Case 3: one production setpoint moves every flow.

  4. Read the analyzers. Case 4: the A feed trimmed every 6 minutes.

  5. Price the pressure. Case 5: 176.1 against 146.3 $/h.

  6. Take the challenge. Two days of upsets inside the mix band and under 195 $/h.

The open-loop shutdown

Case 1 starts at the published state with every valve fixed. The reactor pressure reads 2705 kPa g at 2 h and 2690 at 3 h. Then the imbalance runs away, the reactor cools to 96.6 °C and the pressure reaches the 3000 kPa g interlock at 3.59 h. The derivatives at the start are at most 9.3×10⁻⁴ per hour: this is an instability, not a disturbance. In Auto the same plant holds 2705 kPa g.

Inventory loops only

Case 2 runs the inventory loops: pressure on the purge, the three levels and the reactor temperature (without the temperature loop the undisturbed plant trips at 8.1 h, so it belongs to this layer). At 2 h the A/C step (IDV 1) takes A out of the A and C feed. The A share of the reactor feed falls from 55.0 to 37.0 %, the pressure passes 2900 kPa g at 4.84 h and trips at 5.84 h. With the decentralized structure the share never falls below 45.4 % and the pressure peaks at 2751 kPa g.

A production ramp

Case 3 ramps the production setpoint down 15 % over 2 h from 2 h. The production index reaches 84.6 % and the production 19.29 m³/h against 19.51 by 12 h. The stripper level, the buffer the production loop draws on, swings between 28.7 and 81.1 %. With the stripper level gain at four times Ricker's the level stays between 42.3 and 63.6 %, and the production strays up to 0.55 m³/h from its setpoint in force instead of 0.32.

The analyzers

Case 4 fires the A/C step at 1 h. The reactor feed analyzer's A share bottoms at 45.29 % at 3.30 h and is back at 56.89 % by 12 h, with the A feed raised from 0.251 to 0.595 kscmh, the pressure peaks at 2752 kPa g. With both composition trims at zero gain the A share falls to 22.94 % and the pressure reaches 2981 kPa g within 12 h.

The cost of a random feed

Case 5 runs the random feed composition (IDV 8). Over 24 h the mean cost is 176.1 $/h against 170.7 undisturbed. At a 2800 kPa g pressure setpoint the same feed costs 146.3 $/h, with the pressure up to 2827 kPa g.

A slow drift

Case 6 drifts the reaction kinetics (IDV 13) from 1 h. The structure holds 48 h, but the pressure ranges from 2566 to 2854 kPa g and the mean cost is 209.1 $/h. The inventory loops alone also hold, at 212.3 $/h: the pressure loop and its purge carry the drift.

A sticking valve

Case 7 makes the reactor cooling valve stick (IDV 14). Without stiction the loop holds 120.39–120.41 °C. With it the reactor cycles between 120.09 and 120.71 °C with a period near 4.8 min. A gain of 4 makes the cycle ±0.57 °C at about 8 min, a gain of 16 ±0.23 °C at about 3 min. The reactor pressure stays between 2702 and 2708 kPa g throughout, far from the override, which is why the override has a case of its own with a lost feed.

A lost feed and the override

Case 8 loses the A feed (IDV 6) at 1 h. Most of the A still comes with the A and C feed, but in a fixed ratio to C, so the A share can no longer be trimmed. As shipped the production loop, whose trim scales the product draw with the feeds in this structure, drains the stripper to its interlock at 8.40 h. With the production setpoint at 17 m³/h and the override (this app's design, not Ricker's: above 2950 kPa g it takes 2 % of the production index per kPa of excess every second, and the cuts add up to the production loop's 30-point limit) the plant holds 24 h (5.88 m³/h over the last 4 h, pressure up to 2974 kPa g). Without the override it trips on high pressure at 13.34 h. The inventory loops alone ride the loss at nearly full rate, 21.94 m³/h, but at 408 $/h and 2922 kPa g, and Ricker's own 1996 strategy, whose production trim moves only the feeds, held 20.8 m³/h at the base setpoint in his archived recording. From Ricker's maximum-production point every structure in this app trips within a day, with or without a rate cut.

A grade change

Case 9 moves the mix from 50/50 to 10/90 G/H by mass. With Ricker's ramp (about 2.1 mol % per h) G reaches within 2 points of 10 % at 19.8 h and dips to 7.6 %. At 10 mol % per h it gets there at 5.7 h and dips to 4.6 %. The MPC gets there at 21.0 h with a lowest G of 9.2 %.

The challenge

The target: no shutdown, G within ±5 points of 50 % by mass from 4 h, mean production at least 98 % of setpoint and mean cost at most 195 $/h. As shipped (inventory loops, 2705 kPa g) the plant trips at 6.68 h. The decentralized structure at 2705 kPa g stays up but costs 215.7 $/h. The suggested settings (decentralized with override, 2800 kPa g) hold 48 h with G within 2.33 points of 50 %, production at 99.1 % of setpoint and a mean cost of 187.3 $/h. The pressure peaks at 2944 kPa g after the A/C step, and the status strip warns of the interlock for about 44 minutes.

Scenarios

Each scenario is a complete set-up: mode, setpoint, tuning, disturbances and timed events. Selecting one from the list resets the run, so a scenario is reproducible and can be assigned as-is.

#ScenarioWhat it sets up and what to look for
1Every valve fixed: the open-loop shutdownSets: MANUAL
Look for: A chemical plant at its design point, with nothing upsetting it. Every one of its twelve valves is frozen where the design put it: no controller is running.
Why it matters: A plant's design point is a balance of flows, and a balance can be unstable: the smallest imbalance can grow on its own. Knowing whether a plant holds itself is the first question of plantwide control, before any loop is tuned.
2Inventory loops onlySets: AUTO · Structure (in Auto) = Inventory loops only · at t = 2 h: IDV(1) A/C feed ratio step
Look for: The plant now runs on its inventory loops alone: reactor pressure on the purge, the three levels on their liquid outlets and the cooling, the reactor temperature on its cooling water. The four feeds stay where their stations put them. At 2 h the A and C feed changes its composition (IDV 1).
Why it matters: Inventory loops make a plant stable. They do not make it produce what the owner wants. When a feed changes, someone has to move the other feeds, and an inventory loop only moves the outlet of the vessel it watches.
3Production −15 % over 2 hoursSets: AUTO · Production setpoint ramp limit = 1.72 m³/h per h · at t = 2 h: Production setpoint −15 % (ramped over 2 h)
Look for: At 2 h the production setpoint starts down by 15 %, ramped over two hours. Every flow in the plant has to follow it.
Why it matters: A plant's owner changes the rate many times a year. With one production handle the operator changes one number and the structure moves every feed and draw in proportion. Without one, a rate change is twelve valve moves in the right order.
4A/C feed ratio step (IDV 1) and the analyzersSets: AUTO · at t = 1 h: IDV(1) A/C feed ratio step
Look for: At 1 h the A and C feed changes composition (IDV 1): less A, more C. The reactor feed analyzer reports every six minutes what it sampled six minutes before.
Why it matters: Most composition measurements in a chemical plant are sampled analyzers: gas chromatographs with a cycle time and a sample system. A loop built on one sees a staircase, late.
5Random feed composition (IDV 8) and the cost of noiseSets: AUTO · IDV(8) A, B, C feed composition (random) = On
Look for: The A, B and C content of the A and C feed now wanders at random (IDV 8), all the time. The cost trend shows what each hour of operation costs.
Why it matters: Feed quality is never constant. A structure that holds the plant through it still pays for it, mostly through the purge, and the operating point decides how much.
6Slow kinetic drift (IDV 13) over two daysSets: AUTO · at t = 1 h: IDV(13) reaction kinetics drift
Look for: From 1 h the rates of the two product reactions drift slowly and at random (IDV 13), as a catalyst's activity might.
Why it matters: Slow drifts are the disturbances a feedback structure is best at, and the ones that hide longest: the loops move to compensate and every measurement looks fine while the operating point wanders.
7Sticking reactor cooling valve (IDV 14)Sets: AUTO · at t = 1 h: IDV(14) reactor cooling water valve sticks
Look for: At 1 h the reactor cooling water valve starts to stick (IDV 14): it holds its position until the command has moved more than 2 % from where it stopped, then jumps.
Why it matters: Valve stiction is the most common cause of a cycling loop in process plants. The loop is not unstable and no tuning makes the valve smooth. The cycle comes from the valve's dead band.
8A feed lost (IDV 6) and the pressure overrideSets: AUTO · at t = 1 h: IDV(6) A feed lost
Look for: At 1 h the A feed (stream 1) is lost completely (IDV 6). Most of the A still arrives with the A and C feed, but in a fixed ratio to C, so nothing can trim the A share of the reactor feed any more.
Why it matters: A lost feed is the upset an override exists for: not every setpoint can still be met, and the protective layer has to give up production to keep the plant inside its limits until operators restore the feed.
9Product mix 50/50 → 10/90Sets: AUTO · at t = 1 h: G in product setpoint 11.7 mol % (10/90 by mass)
Look for: At 1 h the G in product setpoint moves from 53.7 to 11.7 mol %: from 50/50 to 10/90 G/H by mass. The product analyzer reports G every quarter of an hour, a quarter of an hour late.
Why it matters: Product grade changes are routine in a multiproduct plant, and every hour of transition is product out of specification. How fast the mix can be moved is set by how fast the plant can be measured.

Look for: Two days of operation: the feed composition wanders from the start, the A/C ratio steps at 2 h, and the production setpoint goes up 10 % at 24 h. As shipped the plant runs on its inventory loops at the base pressure, 2705 kPa g.
Why it matters: This is the benchmark's question in small: keep the plant inside its limits, make the product the market wants, and do it cheaply. Each of the three asks for a different layer of the structure.

Reading the charts and the scorecard

All strips share one time axis in hours, which strips show depends on the view.

  • Reactor pressure and its interlock: the measured pressure, its setpoint, the override threshold and the 3000 kPa g interlock.
  • Reactor, separator and stripper levels: the three transmitters, in their own percent.
  • Reactor temperature with its setpoint, Reactor and condenser cooling water with the reactor valve's stem.
  • Operating cost, now and (dashed) its mean since t = 0, and, on the cost view, its four terms with the same mean, Product G as the analyzer reports it, its setpoint and the delay-free truth, and Production against its setpoint.

The scorecard adds the cost now and its mean since t = 0 (under a random feed the cost swings widely from minute to minute, so compare runs by the mean), the pressure margin, the nearest level limit and the G/H split, and in the challenge what the target is judged on so far. All 41 measurements, the 12 valve commands and positions and the true values are in the CSV.

The MPC faceplate (Advanced, with Structure on MPC over the decentralized loops). The MPC card's MPC move suppression wΔu (0 to 100, 5) is the faceplate's move suppression, and it gains the MPC prediction horizon, read in hours (shipped 4 h, 80 steps of 3 min, 2 to 8 h). There is no band price: this MPC has hard limits only, the boxes and ramp limits of its two handles. Changes take effect at the next plan. Reset keeps them and reloading the case restores them. While the MPC is in charge every strip keeps the right-hand part of the window for the future, the shipped horizon's share of it and never more than a third or less than a tenth (a third of the windows up to 12 h, a sixth of 24 h, a tenth of 48 h): the plan whose first move was applied is drawn dashed after a "now" line: the production and the G analyzer reading it predicts (their bias corrections included), its two handles, the production and mix targets, have no strip of their own, labeled MPC plan, and it disappears in Manual, while another loop holds the handle, or on a fallback. The Constraint prices lines (on the MPC card, Cost view, while the MPC is selected) give, for each limit the accepted plan presses on, how much of the MPC's objective would be saved per unit that limit were relaxed (the QP's own multipliers, summed over the plan): per unit of the controller's objective as solved, which includes the solver's small ridge, not money. They say so when no limit binds. Every value in each range was run on every scenario for the challenge's length under the MPC: no plan was rejected, every logged number stayed finite and every actuator inside its limits.

4. Model

Fifty states: the component holdups and energies of the reactor, separator, stripper and compressor header, two cooling-water outlet temperatures and twelve valve positions. Pressures come from the ideal gas law and Raoult's law with Antoine vapor pressures, temperatures from the energies by Newton iteration, and flows between units from pressure differences. The JavaScript port follows the Python port it was translated with to within 2×10⁻¹¹ (relative) over six hours of closed-loop operation under nine disturbance sets, the undisturbed one among them, agrees bit for bit at the base case with Ricker's independent C translation of the code when five constants are rounded as that translation rounds them, and reproduces the base case tabulated in Ricker's archive to its printed digits. The undisturbed open-loop shutdown time is set by the rounding residue: 3.59 h here, 3.47 h with the C translation's rounding, 4.06 h with Fortran single precision.

Modeling assumptions

Every one of these is a deliberate simplification. Knowing which one you are standing on is the difference between a model you can teach with and a model you can design with.

  • The plant equations, constants, noise levels, analyzer periods and shutdown limits are those of the public code, unchanged. The shutdown limits (3000 kPa g, 175 °C and the liquid volumes) are the benchmark's own teaching values, not those of any real plant, where they come from the vessel design code and the safety-instrumented-system study (IEC 61511).
  • Random numbers come from two seeded generators (disturbance walks and measurement noise apart, as in the 2015 revision of Bathelt, Ricker and Jelali) instead of the original single generator, so a run replays bit for bit after Reset. Individual noise realizations therefore differ from the original code's, while their statistics (the sum of twelve uniform deviates, scaled to the published standard deviations) are the same.
  • The analyzers fire on a step count (every 360 and 900 one-second steps) rather than on a floating-point clock. This changes no sample time.
  • The constants are taken as the decimal values typed in the code, in double precision. A standard Fortran 77 compiler stores the untyped ones in single precision. With that reading the derivatives at the base case are 2.5×10⁻³ instead of 9.3×10⁻⁴ per hour at most, and the undisturbed open-loop shutdown comes at 4.06 h instead of 3.59 h. That time is set by the rounding residue, not by the plant: Ricker's C translation of the code, which rounds five constants its own way, trips at 3.47 h with the same 1 s step, and his archive reports about 2 h in Simulink with a variable-step solver. Halving this app's step, or RK4, moves it by less than 0.01 h.
  • IDV(16) to IDV(20) are implemented in the code (stripper, reactor and condenser heat transfer, sticking of four valves, the reactor-to-separator flow) although the paper calls them unknown. The 2015 revision's IDV(21) to IDV(28) and extra measurements are not ported.
  • The decentralized structure is Ricker's, read from his archived Simulink model MultiLoop_mode1.mdl and run at the base-case setpoints instead of his optimized mode 1. The pressure override and the MPC are this app's, not Ricker's overrides. In that model the production index, with the production loop's trim, scales all seven flow loops. In Ricker's 1996 strategy, as the closed-loop recordings in his archive show (base case, disturbance at 1 h, 50 h), the trim moved only the four feeds and two overrides of his own acted. Under IDV(1), (8) and (13) this app's reactor pressure stays within 68 kPa of his recorded extremes, the production within 0.30 m³/h and the product G within 0.81 mol %. The stripper level is where they part (under IDV(8) his stays within 31.7–66.9 %, this app's swings over −3.4–94.9 %), and under IDV(6) his strategy held 20.8 m³/h at the base setpoint (index 92 %, pressure held near 2950 kPa g by his override) where this structure trips on the stripper level at 8.40 h.
  • The pressure override integrates: while the filtered pressure is above its threshold it takes Kovr % of Fp per kPa of excess off the index every second, and each cut stays in the production loop's output (external reset), which wins production back at its own integral rate once the pressure is below the threshold. In case 8 at 17 m³/h, with the production loop frozen, a filtered excess of at most 1.11 kPa took 41.7 points off Fp within 3 min. The cuts add up only until that output reaches its lower limit (30 points of Fp below the setpoint's index), beyond it the override cuts in proportion to the present excess alone. Over the last 4 h of case 8 at 17 m³/h the production loop sat at that limit 98 % of the time and the override acted 58 % of the time, with the filtered pressure on average 9.85 kPa above 2950 kPa g while it did.

A+C+D→G,A+C+E→H,A+E→F,3D→2F

G and H are the products, F is the by-product, and B is an inert that enters with the A and C feed and leaves only through the purge.

Reactor, separator, stripper and the compressor header each carry eight component holdups (the reactor and separator store A, B, C as vapor and D–H as liquid) and an energy. The two cooling-water outlet temperatures and the twelve valve positions complete the 50 states.

The non-condensables obey the ideal gas law in the vapor space. The condensables follow Raoult's law with Antoine vapor pressures. Temperature comes from each unit's energy by Newton iteration on a polynomial enthalpy.

Pressure differences drive the reactor feed, the reactor effluent and the purge. The compressor follows a curve in its pressure ratio less what its recycle valve returns.

Valves

Under IDV(14), (15) and (19) a valve holds its last command until the new one differs by more than 2 %: sticking.

yk+1​=yk​+Δtf(yk​,tk​),Δt=1 s

As temain.f integrates it. TEFUNC also advances the disturbance walks and the analyzers when it is called, so it is called once per step, never inside a higher-order integrator.

Every flow setpoint is a ratio times the production index, so the production loop moves the whole plant together. The inventory and composition loops set the ratios.

Numerical method and timing

The states are integrated with fixed-step fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) at a fixed step of 1 s. The controller is a sampled digital controller executing every 1 s (one physics step), so it sees the plant only at those instants, the same discretization a real DCS or PLC imposes.

Samples are written to the trends, the scorecard and the CSV file once every 1 min. The physics step is never tied to the browser frame rate: the speed control (0.05, 0.25, 1, 2, 4 h per second, default 0.25) changes how much simulated time passes per real second, not the step size, so a run at 4 h per second produces exactly the same numbers as a run at 0.05 h per second. The trend window defaults to 8 h and can be set to 1 h, 2 h, 4 h, 8 h, 12 h, 24 h, 2 d.

How the model is checked

This app ships with algebraic and simulation checks that run in the project's verification suite on every build. They are worth reading as a statement of what the model is claimed to get right:

  • base case: the 41 measurements at t = 0 match Ricker's tabulated base case to its printed digits
  • base case: the 12 valves equal the tabulated base-case XMV to their printed digits
  • equilibrium: every derivative at the base case is below 1e-3 per hour (Ricker's criterion)
  • open loop: every valve fixed at the base case, the reactor pressure reaches the 3000 kPa interlock (case 1, 3.59 h)
  • each IDV moves the measurement it is named for (open loop, 0.5 h, against the undisturbed run)
  • sticking valves: under IDV(14), (15) and (19) a valve holds until its command moves more than 2 %
  • analyzers: feed and purge every 360 s reporting the sample of 360 s before, product every 900 s reporting 900 s before
  • operating cost: the base case gives the cost of Ricker's tabulated base case within 0.3 $/h (170.6 against 170.8)
  • port against the Python port: six hours, four PI loops, IDV 8 / 14 / 16-20 from 0.5 h, within 1e-9 relative
  • actuator signs: more purge lowers the reactor pressure, more reactor cooling lowers its temperature, more liquid draw lowers the separator and stripper levels
  • controller direction: pressure above setpoint raises the purge. Reactor temperature above setpoint opens the cooling water
  • decentralized structure holds the base case for 48 h with no disturbance (pressure within 15 kPa, levels within 5 points)
  • honest infeasible: the A feed lost (IDV 6) at Ricker's maximum-production point trips every structure within 24 h, rate cut or not (logged sweep)
  • override earns its keep: with the A feed lost and production cut to 17 m³/h, the override holds 24 h and its absence trips on pressure
  • override law (Stage 4, T2): with the production loop frozen, each second takes K_ovr × the filtered excess off Fp, and the cuts add up until the loop's −30 % limit
  • licenses (Stage 4, T3): the Model dialog carries both notices in full, since the shipped bundle drops the source comment
  • status strip (Stage 4, T5): while the override acts it says so, ahead of the straight-line time to the interlock
  • challenge: the shipped settings miss the 48 h target
  • challenge: the suggested settings meet the 48 h target
  • MPC: solves at every decision over 12 h of the A/C step and holds production within 0.5 m³/h of its setpoint on average

5. Limitations

A browser simulation is a teaching instrument, not a plant model. These are the places where this one stops describing the real thing. Read them before quoting a number from it in a design.

What the port changes

  • Random numbers come from two seeded generators (disturbance walks and measurement noise apart, as in the 2015 revision) instead of the original single generator, so runs replay bit for bit. The noise has the published distribution but not the original realizations.
  • The constants are the decimal values typed in the code, in double precision, as the Python port reads them. A standard Fortran 77 compiler stores the untyped ones in single precision, which moves the undisturbed open-loop trip from 3.59 to 4.06 h.
  • The 2015 revision's extra disturbances (IDV 21–28) and measurements are not ported.
  • The Fortran of the Braatz archive is distributed under the University of Illinois notice and the Python port it was translated with is declared BSD-3-Clause. The Model dialog (Provenance and licenses) reproduces both in full, and the port's source keeps them at its head. No Fortran, Python or compiled code ships with the app.

What the control layer is and is not

  • The decentralized structure is Ricker's, read from his archived Simulink model, at the base-case setpoints. His overrides are not reproduced. The pressure override and the MPC are this app's.
  • In the archived model the production index, with the production loop's trim, scales all seven flow loops. In Ricker's 1996 strategy, as the closed-loop recordings in his archive show, the trim moved only the four feeds. Under IDV(1), (8) and (13) this app's pressure, production and product G stay close to his recordings (pressure within 68 kPa of his extremes, production within 0.30 m³/h, G within 0.81 mol %), but its stripper level swings wider (−3.4 to 94.9 % under IDV(8) against his 31.7 to 66.9 %), and under IDV(6) at the base setpoint his strategy held 20.8 m³/h where this one trips at 8.40 h.
  • The pressure override integrates: while the filtered pressure is above its threshold it takes the override gain, in % of the production index per kPa of excess, off the index every second. The cuts add up to the production loop's 30-point limit, beyond which only the present excess counts, and the production loop wins production back at its own integral rate.
  • The MPC models production and G as two separate first-order responses identified on this plant. Cross effects are absorbed by an output bias.
  • The shutdown limits and prices are the benchmark's teaching values.

Why port the code rather than rewrite the model

The value of a benchmark is that everyone runs the same plant. The port keeps every equation and constant of the published code, so a result here can be compared with the literature. What the app adds is the control structures, the scene and the cases.

6. Classroom and practice

Five exercises. All need only the app.

1 · Rank the inventories

In case 1, run in Manual and note which of the six shutdown variables moves first. Then close one inventory loop at a time (using the valve stations to mimic each loop) and find the smallest set that keeps the plant up for 8 h.

2 · Price the pressure

In case 5, run 24 h at reactor pressure setpoints of 2705, 2750, 2800 and 2850 kPa g. Plot the mean cost and the highest pressure against the setpoint and choose an operating point with a 100 kPa margin.

3 · Measure the analyzer delay

In case 4, read the times of the steps in the A share on the analyzer card and the A feed's response. How much of the loop's response time is the analyzer's?

4 · Tune the override

In case 8, with the production setpoint at 17 m³/h, vary the override threshold and gain and find the range that keeps the plant up for 24 h. What does each setting cost in production?

5 · Compare structures by cost

Run the challenge with each structure at the same pressure setpoint and compare the mean cost, the worst G deviation and the production. Which layer of the structure buys what?
